Section 2: Purpose and scope

These procedures apply to late deliveries in the Sidr Square support queue, such as order SD-286.

This document explains how a support agent handles a late order from the moment it appears in the queue until its case is closed: when to open the case, when to escalate it, what to tell the customer, and how to hand it over at the end of a shift. The aim is that customers get an honest picture of their order and that the order is delivered or resolved as quickly as possible.

Out of scope
- Incidents that delay several orders at once, which follow the Dispatch Incident procedure SD-OPS-030.
- Paying compensation, which the refunds team handles.
- Directing couriers on the road, which is the dispatch desk's job.

Section 5: Overview of the case journey

Every Delay case goes through five stages: spotting the delay, opening the case, escalating when needed, updating the customer and closing. Sections 6 to 22 cover each stage and the situations that come up in it.

[Figure 2: Stages of a Delay case from spotting the delay to closing.]

How to use this document
Each procedure section starts with the situation, then the numbered rules, then an example from the field and common mistakes. The numbered rules are what your work is measured against; the examples are only there to explain them.



Section 6: Opening the delay case

When this applies
This starts when an order passes the delivery estimate shown to the customer and appears in the late-order queue.

Rules
6.1 When the displayed delivery estimate is at least 10 minutes past, open a Delay case and record the order ID and last status timestamp.
6.2 Before you open a case, check the order timeline for the last status and the courier's location.
6.3 Do not open a Delay case for an order that has not yet passed its displayed estimate.

Example from the field
Order SD-286 appeared in the queue at 8:40 pm with a displayed estimate of 8:30. Nouf checked the timeline, saw the courier had collected the order 25 minutes earlier, opened a Delay case and recorded the last status time.

Common mistakes
- Opening a case for an order still on time because the customer called early.
- Opening the case without recording the last status time.



Section 7: Reusing an open case

When this applies
This happens when a customer gets in touch about an order that already has an open Delay case.

Rules
7.1 If the same order already has an open Delay case, add the customer's latest message there and keep its original opening time.
7.2 Do not merge cases for different orders into one case.

Example from the field
A customer sent a second message about order SD-301. Khalid found a case opened ten minutes earlier, added the message to it and did not open a new one.

Common mistakes
- Opening a new case for every customer message.
- Changing the case opening time to the time of the latest message.



Section 8: Missing delivery estimate

When this applies
This happens when the order timeline shows no delivery estimate at all.

Rules
8.1 If the order timeline shows no delivery estimate, request the last confirmed status from Dispatch and record that the estimate is unavailable.
8.2 If Dispatch has not answered 10 minutes after you send the request, route the case to the shift supervisor.

Example from the field
Order SD-315 showed no estimate. Reem sent a request to Dispatch at 2:05 pm, had no answer by 2:15 and routed the case to the shift supervisor.

Common mistakes
- Giving the customer a guessed delivery time without confirmation from Dispatch.
- Waiting for Dispatch with no time limit.



Section 9: Escalating stalled tracking

When this applies
This happens when the courier's location or the order status stops updating after the case is opened.

Rules
9.1 If tracking has not refreshed for 8 minutes after the case opens, the support agent must route the case to the shift supervisor. The shift supervisor pages the dispatch desk.
9.2 If the shift supervisor has not replied 5 minutes after you route the case, call the dispatch desk directly.

[Figure 3: Escalation times counted from when the case opens.]

Escalation summary
| Time since the case opened | What you do |
| 8 minutes | Route the case to the shift supervisor |
| 13 minutes | Call the dispatch desk directly |

Example from the field
The case for order SD-322 opened at 9:00 pm and tracking stopped after that. At 9:08 Faisal routed the case to the shift supervisor, and at 9:13, with no reply, he called the dispatch desk directly.

Common mistakes
- Calling the dispatch desk before routing the case to the supervisor.
- Waiting for tracking to come back without escalating.



Section 10: Messaging the customer

When this applies
This applies to every message you send a customer about a late order.

Rules
10.1 Send the customer a status update from the case. Do not promise a new delivery time until Dispatch confirms it in the case.
10.2 Write to the customer in the language they used in their last message.
10.3 Do not share the courier's name, phone number or location with the customer.

Example from the field
A customer wrote in Arabic asking about her order. Sultan replied in Arabic that the order was on its way and gave no new time, because Dispatch had not confirmed one yet.

Common mistakes
- Giving a rough time to calm the customer down.
- Sending the courier's number so the customer can call them.



Section 11: Repeated customer contact

When this applies
This happens when the customer comes back about the same delay more than once.

Rules
11.1 If the customer contacts us again about the same delay, reply in the same case within 3 minutes of the new message.
11.2 Do not close a case while the customer is waiting for an answer.

Example from the field
The customer sent three messages in ten minutes. Haya answered each one in the same case within two minutes and kept the case open until the order arrived.

Common mistakes
- Answering repeated messages with a canned reply that adds nothing.
- Closing the case because the customer keeps asking the same question.



Section 12: Transferring a misrouted case

When this applies
This happens when the order turns out to belong to another branch's queue.

Rules
12.1 If the order belongs to another branch queue, transfer the existing case to that queue with the status history attached.
12.2 Tell the customer that another team now handles the case. Do not share internal queue names.

Example from the field
Mohammed found that order SD-330 belonged to the Al-Yasmin branch. He transferred the case with its history and told the customer that another team was now following the order.

Common mistakes
- Opening a new case in the other queue instead of transferring the case.
- Telling the customer the internal queue name.



Section 13: Cancellation requests

When this applies
This happens when a customer asks to cancel a late order.

Rules
13.1 If the customer asks to cancel a late order before the courier has collected it, link a Cancellation Request and route it to the cancellations desk.
13.2 If the courier has already collected the order, do not cancel it. Route the case to the shift supervisor for a decision.
13.3 Record the reason the customer gave in the Cancellation Request.

Example from the field
A customer asked to cancel an order the courier had not yet collected. Lama linked a Cancellation Request, recorded the reason ("it is very late and I will order elsewhere") and routed it to the cancellations desk.

Common mistakes
- Cancelling the order directly without the cancellations desk.
- Leaving the cancellation reason empty.



Section 14: Restored tracking

When this applies
This happens when tracking starts updating again during an open case.

Rules
14.1 If tracking resumes during an open Delay case, record the fresh status and notify the case owner that the stalled-tracking alert can be cleared.
14.2 Keep the case open until the order is delivered, even if tracking has resumed.

Example from the field
Tracking came back for order SD-322 after a fifteen-minute gap. Faisal recorded the new location, told the case owner, and kept the case open until the timeline confirmed delivery.

Common mistakes
- Closing the case as soon as tracking returns.
- Leaving the stalled-tracking alert in place after tracking returns.



Section 15: Compensation requests

When this applies
This happens when a customer asks for compensation for the delay.

Rules
15.1 If the customer asks for compensation, record the request in the case and route it to the refunds team.
15.2 Do not promise the customer a refund or a voucher yourself.
15.3 If the delay reaches 45 minutes after the original estimate, tag the case as eligible for refunds team review.

Example from the field
A customer asked for compensation after a 50-minute delay. Bandar recorded the request, tagged the case as eligible for review and told the customer a specialist team would be in touch.

Common mistakes
- Promising the customer a discount on their next order.
- Refusing to record the request because the delay was short.



Section 16: Customer cannot be reached

When this applies
This happens when the courier reports that they cannot reach the customer at the address.

Rules
16.1 If the courier reports that the customer cannot be reached, call the customer twice from the case and record each attempt.
16.2 If the customer has not answered 5 minutes after the second call, route the case to Dispatch to decide the next step.

Example from the field
The courier waited outside the building and the customer did not answer. Nouf called twice, waited five minutes with no reply, then routed the case to Dispatch.

Common mistakes
- Calling once and then closing the case.
- Not recording the call attempts.



Section 17: Courier safety reports

When this applies
This happens when the courier reports an accident or a risk to their safety during the delivery.

Rules
17.1 If the courier reports an accident or a safety issue, route the case to the shift supervisor immediately.
17.2 Do not ask the courier to continue the delivery after a safety report.

Example from the field
A courier reported that his motorbike had slipped in the rain. Sultan routed the case to the shift supervisor at once, and the supervisor reassigned the order to another courier.

Common mistakes
- Asking the courier first whether they can still finish the order.
- Treating a safety report like an ordinary delay.



Section 18: Food quality complaints

When this applies
This happens when the customer says the food arrived cold or damaged after the delay.

Rules
18.1 If the customer reports cold or damaged food, record their description and the items affected in the case.
18.2 Route quality complaints to the partner restaurant support queue.

Example from the field
An order arrived after a long delay with cold fries and a spilled drink. Reem recorded the affected items and the customer's description and routed the complaint to the partner restaurant queue.

Common mistakes
- Apologising without recording the affected items.
- Sending a quality complaint to the dispatch desk.



Section 19: Closing a recovered delivery

When this applies
This applies when the timeline confirms the late order has been delivered.

Rules
19.1 When the timeline confirms delivery, send the customer a closure message and record the confirmation time before closing the case.
19.2 Do not close a case that still has an open Cancellation Request or compensation request.

Example from the field
The timeline confirmed delivery of order SD-286 at 9:12 pm. Nouf sent the closure message and recorded the time, but kept the case open because the compensation request was still pending.

Common mistakes
- Closing the case without a message to the customer.
- Closing the case while a compensation request is still open.



Section 20: Shift handover

When this applies
This applies at the end of every shift to every case that is still open.

Rules
20.1 Before your shift ends, add a handover note to every open case you own.
20.2 Assign each open case to an agent on the next shift before you sign out.

Example from the field
Khalid had four open cases at the end of his shift. He wrote a short note on each with the latest news and assigned them to agents on the next shift.

Common mistakes
- Leaving cases in his name after the shift ends.
- Writing one general note for all cases.



Section 21: Peak-time queue

When this applies
This applies when the queue fills up with open cases, such as at dinner time and at weekends.

Rules
21.1 If the queue has 30 or more open Delay cases, tell the shift supervisor so they can add agents.
21.2 At peak time, work on the oldest case in the queue first.

Example from the field
At 9 pm on Thursday the queue reached 34 cases. Haya told the shift supervisor, who added two agents, and the team started with the oldest cases.

Common mistakes
- Picking the easy cases first to bring the count down.
- Waiting for the supervisor to notice the backlog.



Section 22: Protecting customer data

When this applies
These rules apply to every piece of customer data you see in the case system.

Rules
22.1 Do not copy customer phone numbers or addresses outside the case system.
22.2 Do not discuss a customer's order in public channels or chat groups.

Example from the field
A colleague asked Lama for a customer's address to check the area. She sent him the case link inside the system instead of pasting the address in chat.

Common mistakes
- Copying a customer's number into phone notes to call later.
- Sharing a screenshot of a case in the team chat group.

Appendix C: Frequently asked questions

What if several orders are late because of one incident?
Those follow the Dispatch Incident procedure SD-OPS-030, and the shift supervisor will tell you the steps.

Can I give the customer a rough time if they insist?
No. A new time comes only from Dispatch, as Section 10 explains.

Who decides on compensation?
The refunds team. Your job is to record the request and tag the case, as Section 15 explains.
